JTPlay Fire OS Privacy Policy

Fire TV RC 173, Android package com.jtplay.app, version code 3173. Updated November 8, 2026.

JTPlay is a free, independent Plex client. It has no JTPlay-hosted user account, media catalog, analytics or advertising service. It still processes personal data to provide its features.

Data and connections

When you connect Plex, JTPlay receives and uses account identifiers, display names, email addresses where supplied by Plex, authorization tokens, server addresses, library metadata and playback state. It sends authentication and device/app identifiers, library requests, playback progress and searches to Plex and your authorized servers. These connections expose your IP address and ordinary network/device information to those services.

Optional MDBList authorization connects to MDBList for account authorization, tokens, searches and discovery. Optional Seerr connects to the server you choose for account information, searches and request status; submitting a request sends the selected title and associated account information to that server. Artwork, metadata, subtitles and trailer features may contact third-party providers as used. Android WebView or an external browser used for authorization or trailers may store cookies separately and contact the relevant providers.

Connected services, their operators and Amazon process information under their own policies. JTPlay does not control their retention or account-deletion procedures. Use only servers and integrations you trust.

On your Fire TV

JTPlay stores settings, account and server configuration, tokens, databases, cached artwork, playback state, local diagnostic logs and optional downloaded media on the device. Media files and file metadata are read for playback and downloads. Android TV Watch Next integration can share title artwork, playback progress and app links with the device launcher.

Some credentials are encrypted with a key kept in local app preferences. This protects against casual inspection; it is not an operating-system-backed credential vault. Someone with access to both the preferences and database can recover those credentials. Protect your device and backups. Logs can contain account, media or service details.

RC 173 has tracker synchronization, inherited OAuth relays, telemetry and crash uploads disabled or unconfigured. Sentry libraries remain packaged, but JTPlay does not initialize crash reporting. The Amazon build unlocks features without purchases; it has no subscriptions or in-app purchases. JTPlay does not request location, contacts, microphone or camera access, or use an advertising identifier for tracking.

Disconnecting and deleting data

You can disconnect services, remove local profiles and downloads, and clear JTPlay app storage through Fire OS Settings. Clearing app storage removes local app-held sessions and settings and requires fresh sign-in. Uninstalling removes app-private storage; files saved in separately chosen locations and browser/WebView data may require separate removal. JTPlay does not perform these actions automatically on other devices.

For records held by Plex, MDBList or your Seerr operator, revoke JTPlay access and use that provider’s account, privacy and deletion controls. Deleting local JTPlay data does not delete those external accounts or server records. There is no separate JTPlay-hosted account to delete.

Contact

For privacy, local-data removal or support questions, email support@jtmedia.win. If you voluntarily send a support email or diagnostic file, its contents are received for handling your request. Remove passwords, tokens, account details and private server addresses before sharing logs.
